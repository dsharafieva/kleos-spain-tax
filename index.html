The system, in three charges

Three charges. One isn't really yours.

Two of these come out of the contractor's earnings. The third — IVA — usually just passes through, especially when the client is a foreign business.

19–47%

IRPF — income tax

Progressive tax on net profit after expenses, allowances and the social-security cuota. Half is set by the state, half by the region — so the same income nets differently in Madrid than in Catalonia.

Seguridad Social — the cuota

A monthly social-security contribution, not a tax. Since 2023 it scales with real net income across 15 bands. New autónomos pay a flat €80/mo for the first year. It's fully deductible before IRPF.

IVA — mostly pass-through

Spain has no small-business exemption: autónomos charge IVA from the first invoice. But on B2B invoices to VAT-registered EU businesses the reverse charge applies — no Spanish IVA is added, so it isn't a real cost to either side.

A year in the life of a Spanish contractor's taxes

Day 1
Register: RETA + Censo (Modelo 036/037)

Sign up for social security and register the activity with the tax office. Pick a CNAE/IAE code. This can be done online in a few days.

Every quarter
File Modelo 303 (IVA) and Modelo 130 (IRPF advance)

VAT return plus a 20% income-tax prepayment on profit. If clients withhold IRPF at source (retención), Modelo 130 is skipped and the retención covers it.

By 30 Jun
File the annual Declaración de la Renta

The full income-tax return for the previous year reconciles everything paid across the quarters — either a top-up or a refund.

Year-end
Social security regularises the cuota

The TGSS compares the cuota paid against real declared income and adjusts — charging the difference or issuing a refund if the band was wrong.

One contract away from being an employee.

Spain doesn't read the contract — it reads reality. If the working relationship shows dependencia (taking orders) and ajenidad (bearing none of the risk), the Inspección de Trabajo can rule it a disguised employment — a "falso autónomo" — with back-contributions, penalties and reclassification landing on the company. There's no clean 4-of-7 test: courts weigh all the signals together.

Same rate, different net

Cases that change the final number

Two contractors on the same headline rate can take home very different amounts. These are the situations worth knowing before you agree a price.

📍

Which region they live in

IRPF is half regional. Madrid runs the lowest rates in Spain; Catalonia and Valencia can be 3–4 points higher at middle incomes — thousands of euros a year on the same invoice.

Varies
🎉

Their first year (tarifa plana)

A newly registered autónomo pays a flat €80/mo social security instead of the income-based band — often €1,500–3,000 saved in year one, extendable a second year if income stays low.

💼

Already employed elsewhere

Pluriactividad — holding a salaried job alongside — can trigger a partial refund of the autónomo cuota, since they're already contributing through payroll.

🌍

You're a foreign (EU) client

Invoices to a VAT-registered EU business use the reverse charge — no Spanish IVA is added. The 21% simply doesn't enter the picture, which surprises companies used to gross-of-VAT quotes.

IVA n/a
🏝️

The Beckham regime

A flat 24% for inbound workers up to €600k — but classic autónomos billing foreign clients generally don't qualify. It's built for employees relocating to Spain, not freelancers. Read the fine print.

Rarely applies
🏢

Crossing into SL territory

Above roughly €60k profit, an SL can beat autónomo — corporate tax at 19–21% instead of top IRPF. But it adds an administrator salary, extra social security and accounting, so the switch only pays off with real scale.
